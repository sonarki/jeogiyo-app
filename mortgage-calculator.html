Understanding your mortgage payment

Lenders describe a monthly payment as PITI — Principal, Interest, Taxes and Insurance. The principal-and-interest portion is fixed for the life of a fixed-rate loan, while taxes and insurance can change over time. If your down payment is under 20% of the home price, most lenders add PMI (private mortgage insurance), estimated here at roughly 0.5% of the loan per year.

Ways to lower your payment

  • Increase your down payment to 20% to remove PMI.
  • Compare lenders for a lower interest rate.
  • Consider whether a 15-year term fits your budget — higher payment, far less total interest.

Frequently asked questions

What's included in the monthly payment?

Principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, and PMI if your down payment is below 20%.

How much should I put down?

Some loans allow 3–5% down, but 20% avoids PMI and lowers both your monthly payment and total interest.

Can PMI be removed later?

Yes — once you've built roughly 20% equity, you can usually request that PMI be dropped.
