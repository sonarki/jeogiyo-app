How the loan calculator works

This tool uses the standard amortization formula that lenders and spreadsheets use: M = P · r / (1 − (1 + r)−n). Here P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (your annual rate divided by 12) and n is the total number of monthly payments (years × 12). Early on, most of each payment goes to interest; as the balance falls, more goes to principal.

Tips to pay less interest

  • Add even a small extra monthly payment — it goes entirely to principal.
  • Choose a shorter term if you can afford the higher payment.
  • Shop around: a lower APR can save thousands over the life of the loan.

Frequently asked questions

How is a monthly loan payment calculated?

Payments use the amortization formula above. Each month, interest is charged on the remaining balance first, and whatever is left of your payment reduces the principal.

What is total interest?

It's the sum of the interest portion of every payment — the difference between the total you repay and the amount you borrowed.

Does an extra payment really help?

Yes. Extra payments reduce principal directly, so less interest accrues afterwards. This shortens your payoff time and lowers total interest.
